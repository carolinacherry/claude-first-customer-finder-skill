# Research & Qualification Framework

Keeps prospect research evidence-based, current, and respectful. Read fully before searching.

## Product brief

Before collecting a single lead, define:

- product and the outcome it promises
- primary user and economic buyer (they may differ)
- the urgent job to be done
- the current alternative or workaround
- the likely adoption trigger
- geography or language constraints
- explicit disqualifiers

The brief is specific enough when it lets you *reject* a weak match on sight. If it can't reject anything, it can't qualify anything either.

## Query buckets

Search five distinct angles — run them as parallel subagents, one bucket each. Adapt wording to the audience's own vocabulary (how a gym owner and a DevOps lead describe pain differs).

| Bucket | What to look for | Sample phrasings |
|---|---|---|
| **Explicit demand** | Direct requests for a solution | "looking for a tool", "recommend a", "alternative to", "does anything exist that" |
| **Pain** | First-person problem descriptions | "takes hours", "so manual", "keeps breaking", "hate doing", "waste of time" |
| **Workaround** | Duct-tape processes | spreadsheets, copy-paste, VA-run processes, scripts, templates for the job |
| **Switching** | Churn and competitor frustration | cancellations, migrations, missing features, pricing complaints |
| **Timing** | Business events creating relevance | launches, hiring for the role, expansion, new regulation, new integrations |

## Source mix

Spread across source types; a shortlist sourced entirely from one forum is fragile.

Good: public forums and communities, public social posts and replies, product and app-marketplace reviews, GitHub issues and feature requests, public company pages, job posts, changelogs, announcements, public directories and "looking for" boards.

Off-limits: private or gated groups, data brokers, scraped contact databases, leaked datasets, anything whose terms prohibit access.

## Verification rule

Qualify only from the original public page, fetched and read. Not from a search snippet, not from an aggregator, not from memory. Each signal gets: source URL, source title, source type, visible date (or "date unavailable"), and a note of what was directly observed versus inferred.

## Qualification score

Score each dimension 0–5, then weight:

```text
score = pain/5×25 + fit/5×25 + timing/5×20 + reachability/5×15 + evidence/5×15
```

- **Pain strength (25%)** — directness, severity, repetition, stated cost of the problem
- **Product fit (25%)** — how directly the product solves the *evidenced* job (not the imagined one)
- **Timing (20%)** — freshness of the signal and presence of a current trigger
- **Reachability (15%)** — a natural, relevant public or professional contact path exists
- **Evidence quality (15%)** — specificity, source reliability, confidence the signal belongs to this prospect

Bands:

- **80–100** strong first-customer candidate
- **65–79** promising, validate quickly
- **50–64** plausible but missing a material signal
- **< 50** exclude from the primary shortlist

An old explicit request may still qualify — reduce timing and print the date. An ICP match with no evidenced trigger is not a qualified prospect.

## Prospect stages

- **High intent** — publicly requesting a solution or actively switching
- **Problem aware** — clearly describing the pain or an expensive workaround
- **Trigger present** — a current business event makes the product relevant
- **Potential fit** — ICP match with incomplete evidence; keep out of the primary shortlist

## Outreach rules

One opener per prospect, under 90 words:

1. mention the public context naturally
2. connect it to the exact problem
3. describe the product in one sentence
4. ask one low-friction question

Never claim a message was sent. Never include private emails, phone numbers, home addresses, family details, or sensitive traits. Outreach stays a draft until the user separately authorizes sending — and sending is never this skill's job.
